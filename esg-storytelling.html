The Case for Authentic Imagery

There is no room for performative optics.

The world has changed. Sustainability reporting is no longer optional, and the scrutiny applied to it has never been more rigorous. Rating agencies, institutional investors, regulatory bodies, and the public are all looking — not just at your data, but at the images you use to represent your commitments.

A stock photo of a wind turbine, a generic forest, a smiling community that has nothing to do with your actual operations — these are not evidence. In an era of mandatory disclosure and green claims legislation, they are a liability.

I provide something different. Every image I deliver is authentic, geo-tagged, minimally edited, and specific to your organisation, your reporting period, and your actual impact. This is photography as proof — not photography as decoration.

The Problem with Stock

53% of green claims are vague, misleading or unfounded.

That statistic comes from the European Commission's own research — and it is the reason the Green Claims Directive exists. Regulators are no longer willing to accept aspirational imagery as a substitute for verifiable evidence.

When your ESG report uses a stock photograph, you are not just missing an opportunity. You are creating a gap between your stated commitments and your demonstrated reality. That gap is exactly what scrutiny fills.

The solution is not better stock photography. The solution is no stock photography. It is images taken at your sites, of your people, documenting your actual work — with the metadata to prove it.

The Regulatory Context

Why now is the moment to act

The legislative landscape has fundamentally shifted. These are not future risks — they are present realities that every organisation publishing sustainability communications must understand.

CSRD — Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive

Over 50,000 companies across the EU are now required to publish detailed sustainability reports under CSRD. For the first time, these reports face the same rigour as financial disclosures — including third-party assurance. The images inside them must be defensible.

EU Green Claims Directive

Fines of up to 4% of annual global turnover for unverifiable environmental claims. The directive explicitly targets misleading visual communications — not just text. A stock photo of a pristine forest used to represent your supply chain is exactly the kind of claim regulators are targeting.

SEC Climate Disclosure Rules

US-listed companies face new requirements to disclose climate-related risks and impacts. As with CSRD, the standard of evidence required is rising. Photography that cannot be verified is a risk, not an asset.

The Scrutiny Economy

Beyond regulation, institutional investors, ESG rating agencies, journalists, and civil society organisations are all applying increasing scrutiny to sustainability communications. Authentic photography is not just a compliance tool — it is a trust-building asset.
