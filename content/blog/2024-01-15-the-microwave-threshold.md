I am not a social media content creator. I am a conservation photographer. My life — professionally, personally, and spiritually — revolves around the natural world and the stories it holds. Every image I make is an act of witness.

There is a threshold — I call it the microwave threshold — the point at which convenience overrides contemplation. The moment we stop asking *why* and simply accept *what*. It is the moment a microwave replaces a meal cooked with intention. It is the moment a filtered image replaces a felt experience.

I have been thinking about this a great deal lately. About what it means to be human in a world that increasingly rewards the fast, the frictionless, the algorithmically optimised.

Photography, for me, has always been the opposite of that. It is slow. It is patient. It demands presence. You cannot photograph a moment you are not fully inside.

The natural world does not perform for cameras. It simply *is*. And in that isness, there is a kind of truth that I find nowhere else.

So when people ask me why I don't post more, why I don't "build my brand" with daily content, why I resist the pull of the feed — this is my answer. Because the moment I start creating *for* the algorithm, I stop creating *for* the world. And the world, right now, needs witnesses far more than it needs content creators.
