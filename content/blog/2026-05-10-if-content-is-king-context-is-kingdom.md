A single image, no matter how powerful, rarely tells the whole story. Conservation issues- be they environmental, human, marine or wildlife- are inherently complex, intertwined with social, economic and political factors. Though my photographs will often serve as a starting point- a visual hook to draw people in, if my intentions are to truly educate and engage, I need to provide the ‘why’ and ‘what next’. Fail to do this, and I will have failed you, and the subjects of my work.

So what happens when we lack the full picture? What happens when we don’t understand the context behind the content? This is when clarity is crucial.

Let’s consider the content of this image taken two years ago.

This chimpanzee behind bars might elicit interpretations that are in contrast to the truth of the shot. Or they may not. Our immediate reaction may be one of sadness, or even anger. We might think of captivity, of freedom denied. But what if we learned that this chimpanzee was rescued from a situation of imminent danger? What if this place was the chimp’s only safe haven after the loss of its mother?

Suddenly, the bars take on a different meaning. They become a symbol of protection, a boundary between safety and a past filled with threat. The context transforms our understanding and our emotional response. 

**So *why* is understanding context so critical?**
Lived experience has taught me that without context, we are susceptible to judge prematurely, our visceral reactions clouding our ability to seek truth. I have certainly committed my own share of egregious judging. The lessons I learned from those missteps guide much of my life today, and their incorporation into my professional work is great. Though it may be different for us all, here’s why we should all advocate for context: 

* Context prevents misinterpretations

  **.**

   Without it, we risk drawing inaccurate conclusions. We might misjudge a person’s actions, misunderstand a situation, or even condemn something that deserves our compassion. 
* Context fosters empathy. When we understand the circumstances surrounding an event or a person’s life, we are more likely to feel empathy. We can see beyond the surface and connect with the deeper story. 
* Context promotes informed decisions. Whether we’re voting, making a purchase, or forming an opinion, context allows us to make more informed choices. We can weigh the evidence, consider different perspectives, and arrive at a more balanced conclusion. 
* Context encourages us to ask questions: It is a constant reminder that there’s always more to the story. It prompts us to dig deeper, to seek out information, and to avoid jumping to conclusions.

**Truth behind the shot**
The fact of the matter is that this image was taken at a highly regarded wildlife sanctuary. And indeed, this chimp had been imprisoned in a small cage for years, used as a ‘tourist attraction’ and photo bait. When he was rescued from that hideous life, releasing him back into the wild was not a choice- he had no instinct for survival in his natural habitat. 

Is his life better now that he has playmates and an enormous enclosure that enables him to climb trees and hang upside down from the bars? I would like to think so. His playfulness and interactions with other chimps certainly suggested as much.   

This image serve as a powerful reminder. Let’s always strive to see beyond the bars and beneath the surface, to seek out the context, and to approach the world with understanding and compassion.
